The Best Padel Clubs in Valencia: My Honest Opinion

If you live in Valencia or are visiting and looking for a place to play your next match, you've probably noticed that the options are endless. But, as with everything, not all clubs are created equal.

I don't mind driving 20, 25, or even 30 minutes if the club is really worth it. For me, padel is a time I want to enjoy to the fullest, and court conditions are everything.

I've been playing for over two decades and teaching for more than ten years. I've stepped on practically every court in the city, and today I want to share my personal ranking of the best padel clubs in Valencia, analyzing what really matters: clearance height, type of roof, turf, and facilities.

My golden rule: Indoor or Outdoor? In Valencia, it's clear

"I don't play outdoor. In Valencia, when it rains, the court floods and stays wet for days. I haven't played outdoors in over a decade unless it's a mandatory tournament."

The weather in Valencia is great, yes, but for outdoor padel, it presents two serious problems. The first is rain: when it falls, it pours. Courts flood, balls get soaked, and the court is unusable for days. The second problem is night humidity: the glass sweats and the ball slides down the wall as if it had soap on it.

The difference between "Real Indoor" and "Tented Indoor"

Within the indoor padel world, I clearly differentiate two levels. On one hand, we have Real Indoor clubs, located inside industrial warehouses (solid walls, high metal roofs). On the other hand, there are Tented Indoors, which are basically outdoor courts with a tent or canopy placed over them.

As you'll see below, this structural difference completely shapes the playing experience during the summer and winter months.

My TOP 4: The best clubs to play padel in Valencia

Elite 22 (Paterna)

Let's start with the club I've chosen as my home base for teaching, and it's no coincidence. Elite 22 is a real indoor club with 6 courts. What I value most about this club is its high ceilings and the incredible cross-ventilation of the warehouse.

It's true that the locker rooms and cafeteria are simpler and more functional, but they do the job perfectly. For me, it's the go-to place to play in July and August when Valencia hits almost 40 degrees (104°F). It's hot, obviously, but the ventilation saves the match.

Family Sport Center (Beniparrell)

Although it's a bit further away, for me it's one of the absolute benchmarks. It's a spectacular real indoor club with a huge number of courts, very good locker rooms, and a top-tier cafeteria.

However, I feel it has two "tiers" of courts. The center court is amazing, and courts 1 through 9 are of excellent quality with a great roof. But from court 10 to 24, the roof structure has beams running through the middle, losing a lot of height, which penalizes you if you like playing high lobs.

La Calderona (Bétera)

"They have created a great ecosystem. Gym, indoor and outdoor pools... Calderona does a great job as a country club. It's a beast of a facility."

La Calderona is the next level if you're looking for a comprehensive club. They have about 9 indoor courts and 5 or 6 outdoor courts, with excellent clearance height. They have a good restaurant and nice locker rooms.

The weak point: The indoor courts are covered by a tent, not a real warehouse. This makes it extremely hot in the summer, cold in the winter, and sometimes humidity seeps in, making the glass slippery. Also, I'm not a fan of walking out of the covered area sweating and having to walk outdoors to reach the locker rooms.

Sport City (Massanassa)

A macro-club with many courts, most of them tented and a few outdoors. Their facilities are top-notch: a very good cafeteria and excellent locker rooms.

The problem I see is the same one I mentioned about tents. Although they have installed fans to force ventilation, a significant greenhouse effect is created. It protects you very well from the rain, but in summer it is difficult to play due to condensation and trapped heat.

What else do I demand from a good club?

Beyond the roof and walls, there are non-negotiable factors if we want to enjoy the match and, above all, avoid injuries:

  • Turf condition: I run away from courts that look like beaches. I look for well-maintained turf, preferably curly (textured monofilament) with very little visible sand.
  • General maintenance: Taut nets, clean glass (no sweat or grease marks), and spotlights that aren't burnt out or blindingly irregular.

I hope this guide helps you choose your next destination. As you can see, there is no "perfect" club that has it all, but knowing their pros and cons, you can choose the one that best suits your match today.
