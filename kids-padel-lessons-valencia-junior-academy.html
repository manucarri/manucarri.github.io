1. Teaching Philosophy: Sensitive Learning Stages and the "Federer Approach"

In our Sports Science degree (CAFYD) we study a fundamental biological principle that most padel coaches ignore: the sensitive stages of motor learning.

There are specific developmental windows in childhood in which a child's nervous system absorbs certain coordination, balance, spatio-temporal perception and agility skills in a very short time and naturally. If you let that window pass without stimulating it, teaching those same skills in adolescence or adulthood takes ten times more effort.

The "Federer Model" (Multi-Sport Base) vs. Early Specialisation (the "Nadal Case")

To explain how I structure Pre-Padel and junior beginners, I always use the example of two legends: Roger Federer and Rafa Nadal.

The model used by the most advanced pre-sport schools in northern Europe (such as in Finland) is the Federer approach. Throughout his childhood and well into his teens, Federer did not only play tennis: he skied, played football, badminton and basketball, and swam. He built such an outstanding motor repertoire, footwork and spatial awareness that he could have been a world-class athlete in skiing or any racket sport, but he eventually chose tennis.

At the opposite extreme is early hyper-specialisation (the case of Rafa Nadal, focused almost exclusively on tennis from a very young age). In a genetic and mental prodigy like Nadal the result was historic, but nobody talks about the thousands of children left behind under that system:

  • During my university years in CAFYD I shared classrooms with classmates who had been subjected to that fierce specialisation as children. Many ended up as frustrated, psychologically burnt-out athletes: they felt they had to be number one, never reached professional elite level and grew to hate their sport.
  • Worse still: because they had been trained so narrowly in a single movement from the age of 4 or 5, they wasted their sensitive stages for acquiring other general coordination skills, losing the chance to redirect their sporting life towards other disciplines.

Never hand a young child a racket saying: "This is an extension of your hand"

In my Pre-Padel and Junior Beginners classes I radically avoid strapping a rigid racket to a 4 or 5-year-old from minute one so they repeat mechanical strokes.

"My goal with pre-sport is to build coordinated children who have fun and understand the physical world they move in. Before hitting with a racket, a child must understand the physics of how a ball bounces, how to read its trajectory, how to coordinate their feet to move through space and how to throw and catch with their own hands. Once their brain and body master that base, transferring it to padel — or to any other sport in the future — is immediate and natural." — Manu Carrilero, BSc in Sports Science (CAFYD) & Master's in Education

2. An Audit of Valencia's Big Padel Academies: What Parents Should Check in the Fine Print

Beyond the on-court methodology, as a parent you need a transparent model that doesn't mortgage your finances or your family schedule. If you compare our offer with the big traditional schools of Valencia's macro-clubs, you'll find critical operational differences:

A. No 30 € Registration Fee and No Upfront Quarterly Payments

Valencia's big academies systematically charge a "slot management" fee or 30 € registration just to sign the child up. On top of that, they won't let you pay month to month: they force you to pay up front, either four-month blocks of 200 € to 240 € in September or full quarters of 150 € to 180 €, through apps that renew the charge automatically unless you give 15 days' written notice. At my school the registration fee is 0 € and payment is strictly monthly.

B. The Afternoon Paradox: Paying "Peak-Hour" Prices While Your Child Trains Outdoor

A child's after-school time is in the afternoon (from 17:30 or 18:00). At many big academies, afternoon classes are charged at "peak-hour" prices (the most expensive rate), yet they send the children out to outdoor courts to keep their covered courts free for adult court rentals. In Valencia, training outdoors in the afternoon from October to April means cold, wind, humidity that makes the glass slippery and colds. At Elite 22 (Paterna) we train always on a 100% indoor covered court.

C. Rain Clauses at Traditional Clubs: Lose the Lesson or End Up in the Gym?

The fine print of Valencia's outdoor schools usually includes two rules that are very harmful to families:

  • If it starts raining once the lesson has begun: you don't get your money back and the padel lesson isn't made up; they take the children up to the gym for stretches or indoor exercises.
  • If it's cancelled before starting due to rain: many policies state that a lesson is only made up from the 2nd cancellation in the same month (and forcing you to attend at the weekend). In other words: if it rains on a Tuesday that month, that lesson is lost.

In our 100% indoor covered facility, a lesson is never cancelled due to rain, wind or cold.

Frequently Asked Questions from Parents

Why does it matter that the coach holds a Sports Science degree (CAFYD)?
Because a child is not a miniature adult. As a CAFYD graduate, with a Master's in Physical Education and former head of padel methodology at sports camps with more than 300 children (such as La Archila or the IALE Sports Centre), I adapt the loads, the weight of the equipment and the drills to each child's biological stage of growth to prevent joint injuries and early frustration.
Do I need to buy a racket before starting Pre-Padel?
No. Especially between the ages of 4 and 6, buying an unsuitable or heavy racket is a serious biomechanical mistake. In my classes I provide all the specific pre-sport equipment and rackets adapted to their age. When the time comes to buy their own, I advise you free of charge on the right weight, balance and grip size for their hand.
What times are the kids and junior groups held?
Kids and pre-padel groups are mainly scheduled in the first afternoon slots right after school (such as 17:30 or 18:00), offering very convenient logistics with free parking right at the door of Elite 22.

Do you want your child to learn padel while developing their full motor potential on an indoor court?

Age-based groups in after-school hours have very limited slots to guarantee the quality of the on-court stations. Message me, tell me how old your child is and which afternoons suit you best, and we'll see which group has a free slot.
