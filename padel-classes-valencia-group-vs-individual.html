Padel lessons in Valencia: Is it better to train in a group, in a pair, or individually?

Hi, I'm Manu. If you've been playing padel with your friends for a while, competing in some local tournaments, and you feel like you've stagnated, you're probably considering taking lessons. It's the logical step. But when you start looking for padel lessons in Valencia, you find a lot of options and formats: small groups, classes with your tournament partner, individual sessions...

Which one is best for you? As a Sports Science Graduate and padel coach, I see frustrated players on the court every day because they are not progressing. Today I want to break down the pros and cons of each format so you know exactly where to invest your time and energy.

The star format: Groups of 3 to 5 people

If you ask me which is the most interesting and dynamic option, I have it clear: groups of 3 to 5 people.

On a learning level, it's a brutal format. Being a group, it's very easy to replicate real game situations, create a healthy competitive environment, and generate actions where you collaborate with teammates of your same level. Plus, it has an invaluable social extra: you meet other people with whom you can later arrange matches.

Now, I know what you're thinking. When I explain this format to people looking for classes, there's always someone who tells me: "But Manu, padel is played with 4. What do we do being 5 on the court?".

"I don't come to make you play; I come to make you learn. On court, being 5, I can make you hit many more times than in a real match, generating many more learning situations for you." — Manu Carrilero

Why does being 5 on court accelerate your learning?

In a normal match, you hit one out of every four balls (with luck, if they don't play you "the fridge"). In my classes, the goal is not to play a match; it's to automate gestures and make good decisions.

"Five people are always more learning possibilities because there are more possibilities that errors appear in one person that will serve for the other to learn." — Manu Carrilero

Seeing how a teammate makes a mistake and hearing the technical correction makes you integrate the knowledge from the outside before applying it yourself.

Pair classes: Fine-tuning the machinery

The option of training in a pair is very cool, especially if you have a clear goal. It is the ideal format if you come with your usual tournament partner to work on specific tactical situations and understand each other better.

But it's not just for competing. It's also a fantastic option to share a hobby with your romantic partner or a family member (your father, your brother...). Sharing a common hobby while improving your physique and your technique is an investment in health and relationships.

Individual classes: The training scalpel

Finally, we have the 1 on 1 format. Here I focus 100% on the specific needs of your game. There are no distractions.

When do I recommend it? Mainly in two scenarios:

  • Prevention and on-court rehabilitation: If you are coming off an injury (shoulder, elbow, knee, or the dreaded back pain), the individual format is non-negotiable at the beginning. We need to correct firsthand the biomechanically incorrect technical gestures that are hurting you and adapt the intensity of the session. It's not about putting a patch on to play on the weekend; it's about building a solid technique that protects your joints.
  • Millimeter technical corrections: If you are stuck at a 4.0 level and need to improve very, very specific things (like weight transfer in the smash or the bandeja), an individual class allows us to isolate the movement until it is perfected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which padel class format will make me improve faster?
It depends on your starting point. To get ball volume and learn tactics, groups of 3 to 5 people are ideal. To polish serious mistakes or correct harmful habits, the individual format is essential.
Is it effective to have padel lessons with 5 students?
Yes, much more effective than it seems. With fast rotations and constant corrections, you hit more balls than in a match and learn from your classmates' mistakes.
